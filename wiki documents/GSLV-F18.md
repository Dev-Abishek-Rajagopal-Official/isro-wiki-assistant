# GSLV-F18

_2026 Indian satellite launch mission_

The GSLV F-18 was the 20th flight of the GSLV and the 14th flight of Mk2 variant using indigenous Cryogenic Engine (CE).

## Key facts

- **GSLV Mk2 launch**
- Launch: 30 October 2026 23:51, IST
- Pad: Second Launch Pad Satish Dhawan Space Centre
- Payload: NVS-03
- Outcome: Success
- **Components**
- Serial no.: F-18
- Boosters: 4 L40H
- First stage: S139
- Second stage: GL40HT
- Third stage: CUS15

## Mission Overview

### Pre-launch

ISRO began the 24-hour countdown on 29 October 2026 for the spacecraft launch. The mission was to place the satellite into a Geostationary Transfer Orbit at the end of a twenty minute flight. During the pre-flight Mission Readiness Review, ISRO decided to fly the NVS-03 satellite with its pyrotechnic valves already open. This was a major departure from standard safety protocols, necessitated by the fact that the fixes from the failures on NVS-02 was not implemented during the construction of NVS-03, but only from the subsequent satellites. Hyderabad based Ananth Technologies supplied 46 sub-components for the GSLV rocket, supporting a new data-relay package that has been integrated on the rocket.

## Mission Statistics

- Mass: 420 Tonnes at Liftoff
  - Payload weight: 2,250 kg (4,960 lb)
- Overall height: ~50 m (160 ft)
- Propellant:
  - Boosters (4 L40H): UH 25 + N2O4
  - Stage 1 (GS1): HTPB
  - Stage 2 (GS2): UH 25 + N2O4
  - Stage 3 (GS3): LOX + LH2
- Propellant mass:
  - Boosters (4 L40H): 170,600 kg (376,100 lb)
  - Stage 1 (GS1): 138,200 kg (304,700 lb)
  - Stage 2 (GS2): 42,100 kg (92,800 lb)
  - Stage 3 (GS3): 14,400 kg (31,700 lb)
- Perigee: 170 km (110 mi)
- Apogee: 37,023 km (23,005 mi)
- Argument of Perigee: 178° ± 0.5°
- Inclination: 20.75° ± 0.1°
- Azimuth: 106°

This launch vehicle is a 3-stage launch vehicle. The first stage of GSLV was derived from the PSLV's PS1. The 138 tonne solid rocket motor is augmented by 4 liquid strap-ons which is powered by a Vikas rocket engine.

## Payload

The Flight carried the NVS-03 satellite as a payload. NVS-03 is a Second generation navigation satellite, launched as a replacement for NVS-02 that though was launched successfully on GSLV F15, faced an on-board propulsion failure that rendered it inoperable. This payload is operating in L1 band, L5 band and S band. The satellite uses a highly stable indigenous Rubidium atomic clock, developed by Space Applications Center in Ahmedabad to replace the imported clocks used on the earlier satellites.
